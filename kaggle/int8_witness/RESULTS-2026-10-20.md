## What this proves, and what it does not

It proves the 18.8 GB grid plus the interpreter fits a Kaggle session with about
10 GB to spare, and that on Linux the placement's host footprint does **not**
grow during backward the way it did on the Windows box (28.6 GB after load →
44.5 GiB mid-backward, 0.4 GiB free, pagefile thrashing).

It does not prove the real run fits, and it certifies no gate:

- the skeleton is synthetic: no bf16 non-expert parameters (~4.8 GB, device), no
  LoRA, no optimizer state, no 40-layer activation set, and no per-shard staging
  while reading 43 GB of checkpoint;
- each pass is forward-then-backward per layer, which retains the least. The
  real run's gradient checkpointing recomputes inside one backward over the whole
  chain, and that is where the Windows footprint grew;
- bf16 on a T4 is emulated. The 0.6 s/layer here says nothing about a real step
  time, and the 1031 s/step measured on the 16 GB Blackwell card at seq 512 is
  the only real figure either host has;
- one gate in the pre-registration is a claim about a 16 GB card with no WDDM
  spill. A Linux session with a T4 is a 16 GB card, so `memory_vram` is at least
  the right shape here — but nothing in this probe touches it.
